Sequences & series

“Print the first n terms”, “find the sum of the series”, “return the n-th term”. All of these are one loop plus a rule for the next term. This page covers the 4 ways that rule appears. Every problem has a Sequence tab in its visualiser that tabulates and charts each term as the loop builds it.

★How to attack a sequence problem

The routine
  1. Write the first 4–5 terms by hand and number them (i = 1, 2, 3 …).
  2. Find the rule, using one of the four kinds below. If none is obvious, write down the differences or ratios between neighbouring terms.
  3. Pick the loop: for if you know how many terms, while if you stop on a condition (“until it reaches 1”).
  4. Pick the types: fractions → double and 1.0 / i. Big products or powers → long long.
  5. Check against a closed formula when one exists (sum of 1…n, n², AP/GP sums).

1Term from i vs term from the previous term

Both programs below build a sum. In the first, each term is computed fresh from i. In the second, the term variable is declared outside the loop, so it remembers the previous term and is updated from it. The second way is often the only practical one (1, 11, 111 …) and usually the faster one (factorials).

3Traps in series problems

Integer division
1 / i is 0 for every i ≥ 2. Write 1.0 / i, or cast with (double). sum must be a double as well.
Overflow
Factorials pass the int limit at 13! and long long at 21!. Powers and products grow fast, so use long long and check the limits.
Off-by-one in the count
“n terms” starting at i = 1 means i <= n. Starting at i = 0 means i < n. Check the count on the Sequence tab.
Recomputing from scratch
A nested loop that recomputes i! or xⁱ for every term is O(n²). Carry the previous term and it becomes O(n).

ASums & progressions

Each term comes from i, or from a constant step (AP) or ratio (GP).

BAlternating & fractional series

Sign flips, double arithmetic, and carrying a term with a factorial in it.

CBuilding each term from the previous one

The term variable lives outside the loop and is updated every pass.

DRecurrences & simulations

The next term depends on the last two or three terms, or on a position and a direction.

ERun-until sequences

Keep applying a rule with while until a condition holds. Detect cycles with tortoise and hare.
